> Learning cybersecurity is different when you're the one responsible for the challenge.

This week was a little different from the usual routine. I completed my Linux fundamentals and, more importantly, got the opportunity to host **HackVenture**, a CTF competition for students at my college.

## Linux Fundamentals

Completed the **Linux Fundamentals** part of my CPTS prerequisites.

This brings me one step closer to completing the prerequisites before starting the CPTS path.

Next up is **Windows Fundamentals**, followed by Active Directory.

## HackVenture CTF

This week we finally hosted our **HackVenture CTF**.

We had **38 students register**, but only **15 students actually participated**.

Even though the participation was lower than expected, the event itself was a great experience.

We hosted the CTF using a **CTFd server** and created our own challenges instead of simply using existing ones.

The best part for me was seeing how some of the participants approached the challenges.

I was genuinely impressed by some of the undergraduate students. They actually knew what they were doing and were able to approach the challenges practically.

We also had students from **MSc AI Part 2**, since they were from a different department, and one participant from IT.

It was interesting to see students from different backgrounds participate in a cybersecurity-focused event.

## What I Learned From Hosting

Hosting the CTF taught me a lot more than I expected.

When you're participating in a CTF, your main focus is solving challenges.

When you're hosting one, you have to think about everything:

- Challenge difficulty
- Challenge quality
- Infrastructure
- CTFd configuration
- User experience
- Unexpected problems
- How participants approach challenges
- How to improve the event for the next time

Our CTF quality was honestly something I was happy with, and building and hosting our own challenges made the experience even more rewarding.

At the same time, the low participation made me think about something.

I feel there is still a lack of practical cybersecurity exposure in our college. Events like CTFs can help students move beyond just studying theory and actually develop problem-solving skills.

Hopefully, we can improve participation and conduct better events in the future.
